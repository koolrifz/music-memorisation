# Gold records: the big game the whole app could live inside

**Status:** DECIDED in shape, 2026-11-02 (see "The ruling" just below). Nothing
of it built yet. The original brainstorm (2026-10-03) follows it, kept so the
reasoning is understood; where the two disagree, the ruling wins.

## The ruling (Rob, 2026-11-02)

> *"The kids could care less about the pillars. They're only interested in the
> songs... The songs are what's up on the wall. They are the real gold albums.
> ... every level that is achieved is a track... each song before it goes gold
> it needs several tracks to be fixed. And that's what we're here for."*

> *"I think a take correctly done becomes a track. That track becomes black,
> not gold... the ones that have not been accomplished yet need to have an
> icon that shows it's to be accomplished. I don't think it should be a black
> vinyl album... There should be only one place where gold exists and that's
> on the wall of gold records."*

**The story.** Riff and Tango make records. They need the student's help to
make gold ones, and the student has to get trained up first. A melody needs
two things, rhythm (Tango: *when*) and pitch (Riff: *which note*); the student
chooses who to start with. Draft greeting, Claude's words for Rob to rewrite:

> **Tango:** Welcome to the studio! We make records here. Gold ones.
> **Riff:** But every record needs a melody. And a melody needs two things: rhythm and pitch.
> **Tango:** Rhythm's mine. That's *when*.
> **Riff:** Pitch is mine. That's *which note*.
> **Tango:** Get trained up on both and we'll cut a gold record together. Who do you want to start with?

("Music is movement" stays a song line, The Long Hold's theme, not the
greeting's logic.)

**The wall is SONGS, not pillars.** This REVERSES "THE STUDIO WALL: five pillar
records" (2026-10-13). The pillars disappear from the student's view.

- **Five songs, five gold albums** in the studio's background picture: Stomp
  the Beat, Stomp the Beat: Soul, The Long Hold (Rob, 2026-11-02: *"Yes, Long
  Hold is a song!"*), Jam City, Kool Riffs. Five also matches the five Stomp Lab
  albums before 6/8 ("EACH SONG IS A LEVEL" in `CLAUDE.md`).
- **The Long Hold's pitch tracks start at Note Smash** (Rob, 2026-11-02).
  **Open, to check with Rob:** he also said it starts at **quavers**, but it is
  built as Level 2, The Held Beat (halves, wholes, ties; no quavers), and Soul
  is the quaver song (Level 3). Don't move its rhythm levels until he confirms
  which he meant.
- **Tap a record on the wall: a short hook** (about 5 s of that song) plays,
  then they choose it. The hook is a time range in the song's own recording.

**Every level passed is a TRACK on a song.** A song goes gold when all its
tracks are done. Tracks do not have to be played inside the song: passing a
Stomp Lab level or a Staff Smash stage counts. *"You've added to the song.
Let's keep moving. The gold album is in sight."*

- **Each level belongs to ONE song.** If a level counted toward every song,
  every record would go gold together. Rhythm is already allocated (each song
  is a Stomp Lab album). Pitch levels are to be split song by song; Claude's
  starting point, for Rob to rearrange: song 1 Lines and Spaces · song 2 Mixed
  and Staff Numbers · song 3 Note Smash · song 4 Real Smash. Later songs carry
  more knowledge, which Rob is fine with.
- **A later song can be opened early and part played.** Its tracks show their
  names; the ones whose learning isn't done yet are locked (the unlock chain
  already works this way). *"It would be nice for them to be able to see a
  second song and have access to it, but only be able to get through a little
  bit of it."*

**THREE STATES, and gold is only on the wall.** This REVERSES "THE RECORD
LADDER" (2026-10-13), where each level turned gold.

| State | Looks like |
|---|---|
| A track still to do | an **empty album sleeve** (a square), never a record (Rob, 2026-11-02: *"perfect"*). To-do and done differ in shape, not colour alone |
| A track done | a **black vinyl** record |
| A song with every track done | a **gold record on the wall**, and nowhere else |

**Words.** A **track** is a level, and stays done once earned. A **take** is
one go (the app already says "That's a take!" on a clean one). Keep the two
apart: "nail the take, and the track is done."

**Riff and Tango each have a room.** Tango's **drum room** exists (Warm-up
with Tango). Riff's room, at his piano, is where the pitch drilling happens:
*"You better get into the practice room and get some speed on your reading of
your notes... I'll give you a hand, let's go."* That is Riff's tutorial, as the
counting walkthrough is Tango's. **Both names, Rob's call (2026-11-02):** it is
the **practice room**, and Riff tells them musicians call it **the woodshed**
(*"Get into the woodshed"* then means the same place). On-screen spelling:
"practice room" (noun), "practise" (verb).

**Parked, not now:** collecting things to use later, as in the games Rob's son
plays (Resident Evil, The Witcher, Cyberpunk): tracks that can be *used* in a
song. *"We're not doing that yet."* And the melody pads (Zach's idea,
`note-names-in-time.md`: eight letter pads, Riff's melody played in time over
the song) stay an idea until the songs are mapped.

**Rob's order of work:** map the songs; tidy the rhythm levels so they don't get
hard too quickly; then split the levels across the songs.

# The original brainstorm (2026-10-03)

## In Rob's words

> *"I think there could be a nice little place to store songs that have been
> won… Songs that have been recorded. They could be like platinum albums. Up
> on the wall. They try to get more albums. Try to get platinum ones, gold
> ones. Ooh, they get rated. They get the billboard charts. Your song goes up
> in the chart. Anyway, that's just a brainstorm with nothing really attached
> to it. I'm just trying to think of a metaphor."*

> *"How many gold albums can you get? And once we figure out what the big game
> structure is, then everything like the learner's permit will be named
> according to the overall game environment and not something just specific
> to itself."*

> *"If I can make these tunes have five different bass parts, program the keys
> with lots of different variation, we can create some cool music with your
> crappy sounding synths, but it actually works. It's a real game. So we'll
> make game music with cool chord changes, rhythms, and a melody generator.
> That'll be the way to tie it all together."*

## Why it matters

The app has no single place where everything a student has done adds up.
Each game keeps its own stars, medals and permits. **A wall of records would
be that place:** every song finished goes up on the wall, and the question
the whole app asks becomes *"how many gold records can you get?"*

It also answers what to call things. The Learner's Permit, the Artistic
License and the P plates were each named for one game. Once the big metaphor
is chosen, **every award gets renamed to fit it** (Rob's ruling above). Until
then, nothing new is named after the record industry either.

## The shape, as far as it goes

- **The wall.** Every song a band is built on goes up on the wall once it is
  finished (today: when its band is complete). Each record shows the song,
  the parts, and the takes the student kept.
- **Records are rated.** Something like plain, silver, gold, platinum, from
  how well the song was played: the kept takes' scores, the steps' pass rates
  and the beat test are all already recorded per player (see "My band" in
  `CLAUDE.md`). Re-recording a part to beat your score is how a record goes
  from silver to gold. That is repetition with a reason, which is the
  doorway (see "REPETITION IS THE DOORWAY").
- **The charts.** A song climbs the chart as it is improved. Not against other
  students (Rob, 2026-09-23: *"Let's not even put them in competition with
  each other"*): the chart is the student's own songs, ranked against each
  other, or each song against its own best.
- **More songs to fill the wall.** Each new song is a tier up (the songs
  proposal, `docs/beat-smash-songs-proposal.md`), with new parts to win:
  Rob's own bass lines, keys parts and percussion, five or more per musician.
  The mixer on My band already shows every part a musician has, so each new
  part Rob writes is something more to play with on every song.
- **Every silo can press records.** A Stomp Lab level, a Value Smash floor and
  a Note Smash stage could each be a track on an album: the album is the
  app-wide Level (see "SILOS AND BRIDGES" in `CLAUDE.md`), and a gold album is
  a Level cleared well.

## Not decided

- **Is the record industry the metaphor at all?** Rob called it a brainstorm.
  Alternatives worth a look: a tour (towns played), a festival line-up, a
  record shop. Choose one before naming anything.
- **What makes a record gold.** Which numbers, and how strict. It must pass
  "only correct taps score": a rating that rises with time spent rather than
  playing well is a bug.
- **Where the wall lives.** On the dashboard, as the home of the whole app, or
  inside each game.
- **How it meets the levels-and-licences proposal**
  (`kool-riffs-docs/docs/levels-and-licences.md`), which still awaits Rob's
  answers.
- **A melody generator** to give each song a tune over the band. Rob named it
  as the thing that "ties it all together"; nothing is designed yet.
