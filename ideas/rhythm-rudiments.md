# Rhythm rudiments: small games at Beat Smash's level

**Status:** an idea, 2026-10-08. Nothing built. From Rob's first classroom
session with Beat Smash (four classes of grade 5 and 6 clarinets).

## In Rob's words

> *"I think it's going to be a lot harder for them than what I anticipated,
> which probably is a good thing because it means I can do lots of
> rudimentary games of varying varieties."*

> *"Just like I need them to come to terms with marching crotchets and
> clapping quavers. You might think that's easy, but very few humans can do
> that well. Like straight away, they have to practice. It's like they're
> rubbing the belly and patting the head."*

> *"I can think of lots of other games along this level of play."*

## What they teach

The skill under the four beat pads: **holding the beat and the rhythm at the
same time**, and knowing where you are in the bar while you do. The research
note (`docs/beat-position-research.md`) says it is still maturing at 10–12,
is easier slow, and is best built beat first, rhythm on top.

## Candidates, for Rob to pick from

- **Call and response with Tango** (`ideas/jam-with-tango.md`): Rob's next
  priority. No notation; the rhythm owned by ear first.
- **March and clap**: the beat on one pad (or a foot, if a tablet can hear a
  stomp), quavers on the other: the two layers, made into a game.
- **Beat-pad echo**: Tango plays a rhythm and the four pads show nothing; the
  student plays it back on the right pads. The place in the bar without the
  notation.
- **Note names in time** (Zach's idea, `ideas/note-names-in-time.md`): one
  step up, the letter names instead of the beat numbers.
