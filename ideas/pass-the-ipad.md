# Pass the iPad: Stomp the Beat as a game for a group

**Status:** an idea, 2026-10-23. Nothing built.

## In Rob's words

> *"If the music is good, once a student is on their iPad and their speaker is
> on, if another kid hears the music they're going to come running over...
> they're going to see the other ones interacting and talking around it, so
> it'll attract kids and then they'll want to know how to get it and then
> they'll want to share it... we can build in little games, not just levels
> within yourself. What about multiplayer games? You get chances to play
> against each other... Keep recording, get through all of the levels, but if
> you miss, it's then the next person's turn."*

## The idea

Social in the room, not online: one device, the speaker on, a group around it.
The game spreads because the music is good and other kids hear it.

- **Pass the iPad.** Players take turns at the hurdles (1, 2, 4, 8, 32 bars).
  A clean take keeps your turn and moves you up; a miss passes the device to
  the next player. The furthest up the ladder wins, or the first to press the
  gold record.
- It fits the way Beat Smash is already played in class (Rob's clarinet
  classes handed an iPad down a line, 2026-10-08).

## Decided (Rob, 2026-10-23)

> *"In the first instance we don't ask their name. We don't care who they are.
> It's just player one, player two. Like Pac-Man. Or Space Invaders... I want
> this game to be able to go in any direction it can and move on a dime."*

- **No names.** Players are Player 1, Player 2 and so on, as in an arcade
  game. Nothing is asked before play starts.
- **A group game tracks nothing per player** beyond the game in front of
  them. It does not touch anyone's stars or records. What to track is decided
  once the game has been played and we know what matters.
- The solo game (and Beat Smash's nicknames) is unchanged.

- **A miss ends your turn** (Rob, 2026-10-23): *"when they miss a rhythm...
  their turn is finished. Next player."* A clean take keeps your turn going.

## Where the next player starts: two ways to play (proposed)

Rob: *"Do they pick up from where the last person left off or did they go back
to where they were before in their own round? Maybe both options are
available."* Both, as two ways to play, chosen before the game:

- **Own ladder** (competitive). Each player keeps their own place: Player 2
  starts where Player 2 left off. The first to press the gold record wins (or,
  for a short game, the furthest up after so many turns each).
- **Relay** (the band together). The next player picks up the very hurdle the
  last one missed. The group climbs one ladder together and wins the record as
  a band; the score is how many turns it took.

Proposed: **one clean take clears a hurdle** in a group game, not three in a
row, or a turn can run for minutes while the others wait. That matches Rob's
own rule for Staff Smash (the rule of three only after a miss), and solo play
keeps its three.

## Still open

- Which of the two is the default, and whether the names "Own ladder" and
  "Relay" are right (placeholders).
- Do the hurdles in a group game use the same song spots as solo, or random
  spots so the next player can't just copy what they heard?
