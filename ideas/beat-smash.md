# Beat Smash (working title): feel the beat, read it straight away

**The design brief is private:** `kool-riffs-docs/docs/beat-smash-design-brief.md`
(revision 1, 2026-09-29). How it was reached, and every parked idea, is in
`kool-riffs-docs/docs/beat-smash-brainstorm.md`. **The brief supersedes this
page wherever they differ**; this page is the one-page version, as
`ideas/README.md` asks.

**In one line (the brief):** a recording studio. Each musician (Tango on drums,
Riff on bass, Riff on keys) is won by one bar three times in a row, two bars
three times in a row, then four bars in one take, reading real notation; the
student then picks one of three ready-made parts. Last, it's the student's turn
in the booth, which earns the Learner's Permit.

## What it teaches

The very first contact with rhythm, played on MPC-style pads, with **notation
tested from the first round**. Rob, 2026-09-28 and 29:

> *"Press and hold for sustain… you literally make them play a half note."*

> *"Any interaction with beat should definitely look like an MPC."*

> *"Their first go, they have their system. The second go has to be the
> correct system… There's no need to spend levels on dice in a funny different
> notation. We just use it to get as quick as we can to music notation."*

**The round:** shake the dice to get a four-bar figure → play it in the
picture the student chose (blocks, dice, counting, a secret code…) → play it
again with the notation underneath → play it in notation alone. Three clean
rounds in a row win a part.

**A level is a groove:** three clean rounds earn the right to **choose** a
part (drums, then bass, keys, guitar, lead) from a library, shuffling through
presets over the band so far. The reading stays simple; the band's parts are
real music and are never played by the student (Rob, 2026-09-29: *"that rhythm
they're learning does not have to be the same one"*).
Four grooves, then **the Gig**: the student plays lead, in notation, over the
song their band makes. The Gig awards the **Learner's Permit**.

Beat Blocks – Rhythm Practice is Rob's reference for the interface's
simplicity, not a design to copy. The dice roll comes from the 1792
*Musikalisches Würfelspiel*.

## Why the app misses it

Value Smash teaches what a note is *worth*; Stomp Lab teaches how to *count and
write* it. Nothing teaches what a beat *feels* like. Garnet's *"what I need is
a step before this"* is the evidence.

## Not decided

- Which pictures go in the first build, and whether "both" (picture above
  notation) stays after the first part.
- Practising a part in silence, or with the band so far underneath.
- Articulation (accent, staccato, tenuto, slurs on pads): its own world after
  the Permit?
- Two half notes in one bar (Stomp Lab's A2 excludes it).
- **The licence ladder** (L plates → P plates → Artistic License at the end of
  level one) changes what Value Smash V1 awards. Direction only; not built.
- The name ("Kool Beat" is the metronome).
